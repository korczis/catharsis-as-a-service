## Why it works

There are two ways of thinking about a painful memory. From an *immersed* perspective, a person re-enters the scene and re-experiences it. From a *distanced* perspective, the person views the scene from outside. Both involve thinking about the same event, but they have different consequences.

## What the evidence shows

Kross, Ayduk and Mischel (2005) asked participants to recall an anger-provoking experience and to analyse their feelings from either an immersed or a distanced perspective. Distanced analysis reduced emotional reactivity and the tendency to recount the event in a rehearsing way, while still allowing participants to understand why they had felt as they did. Kross and Ayduk (2011) reviewed later studies that replicated and extended these effects to other negative emotions and outcomes.

The finding fits the distinction drawn by Watkins (2008) between unconstructive and constructive repetitive thought. Abstract, immersed brooding tends to maintain distress, while reflective, concrete processing can support recovery.

## In short

Returning to a painful experience is not the problem. Re-living it is. Viewing it from a distance keeps the benefit of reflection without the cost of re-experiencing.
