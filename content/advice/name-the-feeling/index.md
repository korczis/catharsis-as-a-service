## Why it works

Naming an emotion shifts processing from an immediate, bodily response toward a verbal, conceptual one. In the process model of emotion regulation, this changes how the situation and the response are represented, which can alter the emotion's intensity and course (Gross, 1998).

## What the evidence shows

Lieberman and colleagues (2007) asked participants to label the emotion shown in photographs of faces, to label another attribute such as gender, or simply to look. Labelling the emotion was associated with lower amygdala activity and higher activity in the right ventrolateral prefrontal cortex, and the two were inversely related. The authors describe affect labelling as a form of implicit emotion regulation: it helps even though people do not intend to regulate.

Naming also makes distance possible. Kross and Ayduk (2011) reviewed evidence that reflecting on negative experiences from a self-distanced perspective helps people make meaning of them without being overwhelmed. A precise label is often the first step in taking that perspective.

## In short

Before trying to release a feeling, give it a name. It costs a few seconds, requires no discharge, and changes what happens next.
