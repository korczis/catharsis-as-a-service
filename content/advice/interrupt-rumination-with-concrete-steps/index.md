## Why thinking harder does not help

Rumination is about the problem, so it feels like work on the problem. In three experiments, dysphoric students induced to ruminate on their feelings made more negative interpretations, were more pessimistic and produced less effective solutions to interpersonal problems than those induced to distract, who performed as well as nondysphoric participants (Lyubomirsky & Nolen-Hoeksema, 1995). In daily life, rumination and negative affect predicted each other from one moment to the next, a loop that can sustain itself (Moberly & Watkins, 2008).

## What changes the loop

The mode of thought matters more than its topic. Watkins (2008) reviewed evidence that abstract, evaluative repetitive thought tends to be unconstructive, while concrete, process-focused thought can be constructive. In depressed patients, concrete rather than abstract self-focus improved social problem solving (Watkins & Moulds, 2005). Reflecting on an upsetting experience from a self-distanced perspective reduced emotional reactivity and rumination compared with immersed recall (Kross, Ayduk & Mischel, 2005).

Containing repetitive thought in time also has preliminary support: two weeks of a daily, time- and place-restricted 30-minute worry period reduced worry, anxiety, negative affect and insomnia more than a control condition in people with high trait worry (McGowan & Behar, 2013). In clinical settings, adding rumination-focused cognitive-behavioural therapy to usual care improved residual depression in a small trial (Watkins et al., 2011).

## How this differs from brooding to next steps

The advice on [turning brooding into next steps](@/advice/from-brooding-to-next-steps/index.md) focuses on problem solving. This entry adds the recognition step, the observer perspective and the explanation of why rumination is so easily mistaken for processing.
