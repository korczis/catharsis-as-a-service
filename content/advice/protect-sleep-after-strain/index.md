## Why it matters

Across 154 experiments with 5,717 healthy participants, all forms of sleep loss reduced positive affect and increased anxiety symptoms (Palmer et al., 2024). A second set of meta-analyses found that sleep loss increased negative mood and markedly decreased positive mood, and that sleep restriction modestly reduced adaptive emotion regulation in young people (Tomaso, Johnson & Nelson, 2021). After one night without sleep, healthy adults reported more stress, anxiety and anger than rested controls in response to a mild stressor, consistent with a lowered threshold for perceiving stress (Minkel et al., 2012).

This means that staying up late to resolve an emotional strain can work against the aim. The next day begins with fewer positive resources and a shorter fuse, and the strain is likely to feel larger.

## What better sleep is associated with

Across 65 randomised trials, interventions that improved sleep also improved composite mental health, depression, anxiety and rumination, with greater sleep improvement linked to greater benefit (Scott et al., 2021). Over longer periods, insomnia was associated with about twice the odds of later depression (Baglioni et al., 2011), though that association cannot show direction.

## Why the steps are modest

Individual sleep hygiene recommendations are associated with better sleep, but their direct effects in the general population remain largely untested (Irish et al., 2015). The steps above are therefore low-risk suggestions consistent with the evidence, not a validated protocol.
