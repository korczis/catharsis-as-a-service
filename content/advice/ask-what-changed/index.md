## Why it matters

Any behaviour that reliably removes an unpleasant internal state is negatively reinforced and becomes more likely. This is adaptive when the behaviour also deals with the source of the state and self-perpetuating when it does not. Hayes and colleagues (1996) described experiential avoidance, the attempt to escape or change unwanted internal experiences even when this causes harm over time, as a process common to many psychological difficulties.

## Why memory makes it harder to see

Retrospective evaluations of experiences are dominated by their most intense moment and by how they end, while their duration is largely neglected (Fredrickson & Kahneman, 1993). In one demonstration, participants chose to repeat a longer unpleasant experience that ended slightly better over a shorter one (Kahneman et al., 1993). A strong peak and a good ending make an evening remembered as more restorative than its lasting effect justifies.

## What the review does

The four questions in this entry separate the effect of an experience on feeling from its effect on understanding, decisions and actions. They do not judge the experience. They make visible whether relief is standing in for resolution, which is the pattern the artifact describes as `problem_solved: false`.
